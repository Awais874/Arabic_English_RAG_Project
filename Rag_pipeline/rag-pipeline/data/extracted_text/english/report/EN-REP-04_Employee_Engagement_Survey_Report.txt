2026 Response Rate: 82% (1,148 of 1,400 employees) Distribution: Internal —
Department Heads and Executive Committee
1. Executive Summary
The 2026 Employee Engagement Survey recorded an overall engagement score of
74%, an improvement of five percentage points on the 2025 result. The strongest
scores were recorded for "pride in working for the Company" (81%) and "clarity of
role expectations" (78%). The lowest-scoring area was "opportunities for career
progression" (61%), consistent with feedback from the 2025 survey and now
flagged as a priority area for 2026-2027.
2. Methodology
2.1 The survey was administered electronically over a two-week period in May
2026 to all permanent employees, using a standardised 40-question instrument
covering seven engagement dimensions: leadership, communication, career
development, wellbeing, recognition, teamwork, and pride in the organisation.
2.2 Responses were anonymous and aggregated at the department level for
reporting where a minimum group size of ten respondents was met, in order to
protect individual anonymity.

4. Departmental Variation
4.1 Distribution centre employees reported lower engagement on the Career
Development dimension (54%) compared with corporate office employees (69%),
suggesting a need for clearer progression pathways for warehouse-based roles
specifically.
4.2 The Customer Service department recorded the highest overall engagement
score (80%), which the Human Resources team attributes in part to the
department's structured onboarding process and regular team recognition
programme.
5. Open-Text Themes
5.1 Analysis of open-text responses identified three recurring themes: a desire for
more transparent internal promotion processes, positive feedback on the expanded
Remote Working Policy (EN-POL-06), and requests for more structured skills
training for warehouse-based roles.
6. Recommendations
6.1 Develop a formal career pathway framework for distribution centre roles, in
consultation with Supply Chain and Logistics leadership, for rollout in Q4 2026.
6.2 Continue and expand the recognition programme piloted in Customer Service
to other departments, given its apparent association with higher engagement
scores.
